It started with my dad's camera

My dad was a self-proclaimed picture nerd, never without a camera around his neck. Years after he died, I finally turned his camera on again and found the last photo he ever took: fall leaves, from our kitchen window.

Through his lens I fell in love with photography. I've spent years trying to perfect the look of an authentic vintage photograph, and each decade asks for something different.

I

The 1950s

A Halloween party in a living room that already looked the part. Grainy, overexposed in places, a little chaotic: the party as dad's old camera would have caught it.

II

The 1960s

A neon pink jumpsuit, a little old Hollywood, and a porch with a pink door. The '60s are where I go when I want color.

Also in this chapter: A '60s look and American Sign Museum.

III

The 1970s

Valentines, a checkered rug and a lace mask, in a sunny living room with a wall of records.

IV

From the page

A lot of my shoots start from a book or a play. "Love looks not with the eyes, but with the mind." Shakespeare, Washington Irving, and a book of poems about Annie Oakley.

V

Halloween, all year

I will always say yes to Halloween, even in August. A glam killer clown, a cemetery at dusk, and the best decorated house in all the 'Nati.
